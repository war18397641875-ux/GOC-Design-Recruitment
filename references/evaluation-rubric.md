# Evaluation Rubric

Use this 100-point rubric to make decisions more consistent. The score structures judgment; it is not a validated psychometric test and must not be treated as objective truth. Always show the evidence and apply the hard gates after calculating the total.

## Default role profile

Unless the user provides a different job brief, assume the GOC junior/graduate visual designer role discussed in this skill's source context: commercial brand communication and overseas-facing work, including campaign key visuals, digital ads, e-commerce assets, landing pages/web visuals, and social content. For this role, **basic AIGC design ability is a must-have gate**; AI video, LoRA/ComfyUI workflows, coding, and advanced automation are bonuses unless specifically listed as essential. For other employers/roles, extract must-haves and bonuses from their job brief instead of inheriting GOC requirements. State the assumed profile when no JD is provided.

### Basic AIGC gate for the default GOC role

The candidate should be able to use at least one generative image workflow (such as text-to-image or image-to-image) for a design task, select and iterate results, make or direct a targeted correction/composite, and explain how they judge whether the output is usable. Do not require a particular vendor or advanced model workflow unless the JD says so. A tool-name list or one attractive generated image is not enough evidence of competence.

## Choose the level before scoring

Use the level stated in the job brief. If absent, classify by expected responsibility, not years alone, and state the assumption. Do not combine thresholds across levels. These are practical screening calibrations, not empirically validated tests.

| Level | Typical scope | Weights A/B/C/D/E | Advance / reserve bands |
|---|---|---|---|
| 应届 | Primarily learning; handles guided assignments and contributes scoped pieces | 30 / 25 / 15 / 20 / 10 | Advance ≥75; reserve 65–74; below 65 do not advance |
| 初级 | Independently completes clearly scoped design tasks, follows an existing brief/system, and handles ordinary revisions | 27 / 23 / 17 / 21 / 12 | Advance ≥75; reserve 65–74; below 65 do not advance |
| 中级 | Independently owns ordinary projects end-to-end, makes and explains design decisions, coordinates constraints/stakeholders, and delivers consistently | 22 / 18 / 15 / 25 / 20 | Advance ≥78; reserve 68–77; below 68 do not advance |

Weights are listed in order A/B/C/D/E and total 100 for each level. Higher levels shift more weight toward role delivery, reasoning, and ownership while preserving visual fundamentals as non-compensable gates.

### Level-specific meaning of a 3/5

- **应届:** Can produce a legible, reasonably controlled response to a guided brief. Some unevenness is expected, but basic composition, typography, and craft must be present.
- **初级:** Can reliably execute a clearly scoped assignment with limited supervision, apply an existing design system, and revise work based on feedback without recurring foundational errors.
- **中级:** Can independently take a normal project from brief clarification through final delivery, explain tradeoffs, coordinate constraints, and maintain quality across formats. A polished single artifact without ownership/process evidence is not enough.

Use the actual job scope when it differs from these defaults. Time in a role is context only.
## Five scored dimensions (100 points total)

Choose the correct level weights above, then rate each dimension from 1 to 5 using the anchors below and calculate:

**Dimension points = dimension weight × rating ÷ 5.** Add the five dimension points for the total. Report each rating and weighted points, for example `视觉审美 4/5 = 24/30`.

## Specialist bonuses (up to +12 points)

After calculating the base score out of 100, award up to 4 bonus points in each specialist area below. These bonuses recognize skills that materially increase this role's value. They are added to the base score and capped at 100:

**Adjusted score = min(100, base score + AIGC bonus + AI-video bonus + motion/animation bonus).**

| Specialist area | Bonus range | What earns points |
|---|---:|---|
| Advanced AIGC control beyond the basic required gate | 0–4 | Repeatable style/product/character control, meaningful prompt iteration, local edits/compositing, robust quality correction, and a clear workflow that yields usable outputs |
| AI video / AI-assisted video editing | 0–4 | A coherent finished clip or sequence with concept/storyboard, shot or character consistency, purposeful editing/pacing, and quality control; generating isolated clips alone earns little |
| Motion design / animation | 0–4 | Strong kinetic typography, 2D/3D animation, or motion systems with intentional timing, transitions, visual clarity, and polished delivery |

For each area, use this scale: **0** no qualifying evidence in the supplied materials; **1** isolated/basic attempt; **2** usable execution with some control; **3** strong, repeatable work; **4** exceptional control and finished quality. A 0 means “not demonstrated here,” not “the candidate cannot do it.” Do not award the same evidence twice: separate AIGC generation/control from video editing and motion craft, and only score an area when its distinct contribution is visible. A candidate can earn multiple bonuses when the work genuinely demonstrates each separate skill.

The basic AIGC must-have gate is separate and cannot be satisfied by bonus points. Apply the level thresholds to the adjusted score, but **never** let bonuses override failed visual fundamentals, a required-format gate, or a missing role-relevant standout. Strong bonuses can move a qualified borderline candidate over the numerical cutoff and increase priority among candidates who already meet the gates.

| Dimension | Graduate weight | Junior weight | Mid-level weight | What to judge |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| A. Visual quality and taste | 30 | 27 | 22 | Art direction, composition, distinctiveness, appropriate mood, purposeful restraint; whether the work feels considered rather than merely finished/decorated |
| B. Typography, layout, and hierarchy | 25 | 23 | 18 | Type choices, alignment, spacing, contrast, reading order, information clarity in actual outputs; for web, long-page rhythm and consistency |
| C. Craft and consistency | 15 | 17 | 15 | Image treatment, detail accuracy, production quality, adaptation across sizes/channels, consistency across multiple projects |
| D. Commercial communication and role fit | 20 | 21 | 25 | Clarity of audience/message/product/action, suitability to brand and channel, evidence for the actual role's required formats and delivery needs |
| E. Design reasoning and contribution | 10 | 12 | 20 | Credible process and iteration, clarity of personal contribution, ability to distinguish original work from team assets, existing IP, templates, and AI output |

Internship count, employer names, project count, and software names do not earn points by themselves. Award points for demonstrated work and specific relevant experience only. AI skill contributes to E only when the portfolio shows the candidate's actual art direction, selection, editing, integration, and quality control; attractive generated images alone do not earn a high rating. Score AIGC as a separate **must-have gate** when the JD (or the default GOC role above) requires it; a high total cannot compensate for an unmet must-have.

## Rating anchors (apply to every dimension)

- **1 — Clearly below baseline:** repeated or decisive defects undermine communication or usability; foundational control is missing.
- **2 — Below the expected baseline for the selected level:** noticeable weaknesses recur; some competent moments, but the work is not dependable at the level this role needs.
- **3 — Meets the baseline for the selected level:** the work generally solves the brief and is usable; quality may be ordinary and have visible areas to improve.
- **4 — Strong evidence:** clear, intentional decisions and above-baseline execution; at least one meaningful strength is visible, with good control in this dimension.
- **5 — Exceptional for the target level:** unusually distinctive, controlled, and repeatable work; strong evidence across more than one relevant example, not just a single polished hero image.

Use whole-number ratings. Do not use decimals to create false precision. Add a short evidence note for each rating, especially for any 1, 2, or 4–5. If a plausible one-point change in any rating would cross an advance/reserve cutoff, label the outcome 临界, show the alternate total, and identify the evidence behind the chosen rating. Do not silently round toward advancement.

## Selecting evidence and scoring consistency

1. Inspect the full portfolio at overview scale. Choose up to three representative projects for closer review: the strongest role-relevant project, another materially different project, and a typical or weaker example when available. This prevents a single hero project or sheer project volume from dominating.
2. Rate the candidate across the body of evidence, not by averaging every page equally. A dimension score should reflect both quality and repeatability. If one project is excellent and the rest are weak, say so and do not rate repeatability as strong. For B, judge typography/layout in actual design artifacts separately from the portfolio deck's master layout. An average deck style should not lower B when the project outputs themselves show adequate control; polished deck layout cannot mask weak project outputs.
3. Cite the project/page or visible element behind each material judgment. If visual inspection is incomplete, lower confidence and do not imply full-portfolio review.
4. Score transferable work when the target format is absent, but state that it is indirect evidence. Do not pretend an unrelated project proves role-specific experience.

## Decision thresholds and non-compensable gates

Apply the threshold band for the selected level to the adjusted score. A total score never overrides a failed core gate.

Before applying the score band, check every must-have extracted from the job brief as a separate pass/unverified/fail gate. For the default GOC role, this includes basic AIGC ability. If a required capability is **explicitly absent**, the candidate does not advance regardless of total score. If the portfolio simply does not show the capability, mark it **unverified** and use 备选 pending a targeted screen or interview check; absence of evidence is not proof of inability. If a skill is only a bonus, do not make it a gate.

### 推进面试

**应届和初级**须全部满足：

- Reach that level's advance cutoff (75/100).
- A. Visual quality and taste, B. Typography/layout/hierarchy, and C. Craft/consistency are each at least 3/5.
- If a format (such as web/landing pages) is essential, relevant work in D is at least 3/5 with no repeated clear weakness in that format.
- At least one role-relevant project has a clear standout: project-level A or D is at least 4/5 and the other is at least 3/5. The portfolio does not show repeated foundational problems.

**中级**须全部满足：

- Reach 78/100.
- A, B, C, D, and E are each at least 3/5.
- Show at least two relevant projects with credible personal ownership and consistent delivery; at least one has project-level A or D at least 4/5 and the other at least 3/5.
- Any essential format is at least 3/5 in relevant work, without repeated weakness.

A complete portfolio that only meets expectations is not by itself a reason to spend an interview slot.

### 备选

- **应届 / 初级:** 65–74/100 with no hard-gate failure and credible relevant promise; or 75+ where the standout, consistency, attribution, or essential-format evidence is specifically uncertain.
- **中级:** 68–77/100 with no hard-gate failure and evidence of potential ownership; or 78+ where a specific uncertainty (such as attribution or repeatability) prevents a confident advance decision.

Name the exact uncertainty. “备选” is not a polite substitute for a demonstrated failure.

### 不推进

- Below the selected level's reserve band (under 65 for graduate/junior; under 68 for mid-level).
- A or B is 1–2/5, or C is 1–2/5 because fundamental execution problems recur.
- An essential role format is clearly below baseline.
- The numeric advance cutoff is met but required level-specific project evidence is absent. Use “备选” only when there is a specific unresolved uncertainty; if the work is clearly competent but has no relevant standout, do not advance.

State which condition determined the outcome. Never let project volume, internship count, named employers, AI tools, or polished packaging compensate for a failed gate.
## Examples of interpretation

- A graduate e-commerce portfolio with good completion but flat typography/color and no standout may score around the baseline yet still fail the standout condition; do not advance based solely on completion. When the portfolio deck itself feels plain but later IP/brand deliverables show stronger art direction and readable internal layouts, rate visual quality and B from those underlying examples too; do not let a weak opening project define the whole portfolio.
- A candidate with one excellent campaign but weak web pages should not pass if web is an essential part of the role. If web is only a bonus, explain the gap and assess the relevant core work instead.
- An existing brand/IP extension may earn credit for application and art direction, but not for original identity/character creation unless the candidate shows that original authorship.
- A beautifully packaged case study does not raise the underlying project ratings. Judge the actual artifacts separately from the deck. Conversely, an average portfolio-wide template should not lower the typography/layout score when the actual project outputs show adequate control.

## Evidence notes

Use concise, concrete observations, such as:

- “电商活动页信息量大，但主促销信息仍清楚；后续网页页型的字体层级和版式节奏明显变平，显示长页面控制不够稳定。”
- “该项目沿用既有品牌/IP，作品展示的是角色延展和场景应用；可作为执行与AI工作流证据，不能直接算作原创IP能力。”

Avoid unsupported conclusions such as “不稳定,” “没创意,” or “能力很强.” Replace them with what the portfolio supports and what remains unknown.
